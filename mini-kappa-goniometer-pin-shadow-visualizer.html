Separation is measured between the directions of the ω axis as seen from the crystal (v = Rᵀ·Z), because ω is the sweep axis and rotates every reciprocal-space region about it anyway. Settings that differ only by an ω rotation — including any φ move at κ = 0, where φ and ω are collinear — are therefore identical (0°). The polar angle of v from the φ axis depends on κ alone (cos β = cos²α + sin²α·cos κ, at most 2α) and φ sets its azimuth, so every direction inside the disc of radius 2α is reachable. Find positions looks for two further settings that make an equilateral triangle of side Δ with the current one (1–2 = 1–3 = 2–3 = Δ), in κ and φ only. A minimises the tilt of the sample z-axis from the ω axis, B minimises κ travel (only listed when it differs from A), and S is the fully symmetric trio at one κ with φ spaced by 120°, which has the smallest possible tilt but moves the start position too. Such a trio exists for Δ up to ≈ 80° at α = 24°. The map shows the three ω-axis directions in the sample frame (radius = true angle from the φ axis). Results stay frozen until Find positions is clicked again. The solver does not enforce the κ travel limit.

Goniometer geometry

κ / φ tilt α °
mounting rotation °
sample x sample y sample z lab X / Y / Z

Lab frame: +X is the incident beam direction (towards the detector), +Z is the ω axis (⊥ beam). The whole goniometer is mounted turned by the mounting rotation about ω (default 90°). The κ axis is tilted away from the detector in the un-turned assembly, so at ω = 0 it is tilted α (24°) from the ω axis towards lab −Y, i.e. in the plane ⊥ to the beam; the φ axis is tilted α from the κ axis and lies along ω when κ = 0. All three rotation axes intersect at the sample. ω is right-handed (counter-clockwise about +Z); κ and φ are left-handed (positive angle = clockwise looking along +axis). The sample frame is defined to coincide with the lab frame at all angles zero: sample x ∥ lab +X, y ∥ lab +Y, z ∥ lab Z (it is counter-turned relative to the φ-stage hardware). The Eulerian angles are given in goniometer terms (zero at the motor zero), independent of that choice. The headline separation is the angle between the ω-axis directions in the sample frame, Rᵀ·Z, for the current and reference setting (independent of ω); the full rigid rotation Rcur · Rref−1 is listed separately. Note that κ-zero conventions differ between vendors (sometimes by 180°). The sample normal always stays within 2α of the ω axis — the fundamental mini-κ workspace limit (48° for α = 24°). Shadow view options

The sample is a point at the origin; the pin is drawn 15× oversized for visibility, the φ arm and its disks at true scale. The pin shadow is modeled as a fixed cone of half-angle 19.84° about the φ axis. The φ arm — a 30 mm wide plate 70 mm below the sample bridging the κ and φ axes — carries two ⌀ 30 mm disks centered on the φ and κ axes. Rays from the sample to the detector are traced exactly.

Shadow geometry and math used
  • Shared model. Both 3D views, the shadow map and the readouts use the same ω, κ, φ values, α, mounting rotation and κ tilt (away from the detector). The goniometer view realises that κ tilt direction as an extra 180° turn of its assembly, which is exactly equivalent.
  • Axes. All three rotation axes pass through the sample. Omega is the lab z axis. The κ axis is tilted by α = 24° from z (tilted away from the detector) and turns with ω. The φ axis is tilted by the same α from the κ axis, on the opposite side, so at κ = 0 the φ and ω axes coincide. Rotating κ swings the φ axis on a cone: at κ = ±180° it is 2α = 48° away from vertical. The "mounting rotation" (default 90°) acts as a constant offset added to ω; the κ motor travels only −4° … 250°.
  • Pin. Drawn along the φ axis, nominally 1 mm to 21 mm from the sample (drawing only). Its shadow is modeled directly as a fixed cone of half-angle δ = 19.84° about the φ axis, decoupled from the drawn pin geometry.
  • φ arm and disks. A flat rectangular plate perpendicular to the κ/φ plane. Its midline runs from the φ head (70 mm from the sample along the φ/pin axis, motor side) to the point 70 mm along the κ axis on the same side, bridging the 24° gap (midline length ≈ 29.1 mm); the plate extends 15 mm to either side of the κ plane. Two ⌀ 30 mm disks lie in the same plane, one centered on the φ axis at the φ head, the other on the κ axis at the arm's κ end. The assembly is rigidly attached to the κ carriage, so it rotates with κ and ω. Rays are tested with exact ray intersections (parallelogram and coplanar disks share one plane, so a single ray–plane crossing serves every test).
  • Shadow criterion (exact for a point sample). The pin axis, extended backwards, passes through the sample, so the pin blocks exactly the rays within a fixed half-angle δ of the φ axis: an exact cone. The φ arm and its disks are tested with exact ray intersections. The red patch is the union of these blockers projected from the sample point onto the detector plane.
  • Map. For each 1° × 1° (ω, κ) cell the detector plane is sampled on a 24 × 24 grid and the blocked fraction of detector area is computed with the exact criterion above. The color ramp runs from 0% to 15% of the detector area.
  • Inscribed circle and resolution. The dashed circle centred on the direct beam touches the nearest detector edge: its radius is min(width / 2 − |beam offset x|, height / 2 − |beam offset y|). A point on it scatters through 2θ = arctan(radius / distance); the label shows d = λ / (2 sin θ) with the wavelength set in the Detector & φ arm card (default λ = 1.033 Å, 12 keV): about 45.3° and 1.34 Å for the default Eiger2X 16M at 154 mm.
  • Detector models and pixel mask. Eiger2X 16M (327.2 × 311.1 mm, 4362 × 4148 pixels of 75 µm), Eiger2X 9M (244.7 × 233.1 mm, 3262 × 3108 px of 75 µm), Pilatus3 6M (434.6 × 423.6 mm, 2527 × 2463 px of 172 µm) or a custom rectangle. Width is the horizontal extent (lab y), height the vertical extent (lab z). Beam offsets x (+lab y) and y (+lab z) shift the detector rectangle relative to the direct beam. The mask button traces one ray per real detector pixel at the current (ω, κ) and downloads a grayscale PNG at native resolution (black = shadowed, white = clear): column 0 at the +y edge (left in the sample-side view), row 0 at the +z edge (top). Masks are available for the named models only; any state change cancels a running mask computation.
